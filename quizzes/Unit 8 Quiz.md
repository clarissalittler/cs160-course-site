## Unit 8 Quiz — Data

### Multiple Choice

**1.** A city asks residents to report potholes through a smartphone app and then maps every report. Most of the reports come from a handful of neighborhoods. Before concluding that those neighborhoods have the worst roads, what does Lesson 1 suggest you ask?
- A) Nothing, since every single report on the map is a real pothole
- B) Who's in the sample, since the map partly shows who has the app and time to report
- C) Only how many reports there are, since a big enough pile of reports can't be biased
- D) Whether the pothole reports count as discrete data or as continuous data

**2.** A dataset of students includes a column of zip codes (97214, 97202, ...). A classmate says the column must be quantitative, since the values are all made of digits. What does Lesson 1 say?
- A) They're right — any value made of digits is quantitative
- B) The column is categorical: zip codes are numbers used as labels, and doing math on them (like averaging two zip codes) doesn't mean anything
- C) The column is quantitative and continuous, since there are so many possible zip codes
- D) The column is quantitative and discrete, since zip codes come in whole numbers

**3.** A transit agency's chart compares weekday riders on two bus lines. Line 12 had 50,000 riders and Line 75 had 51,000 (about a 2% difference), yet Line 75's bar is drawn three times as tall as Line 12's. Which chart trick from Lesson 2 is most likely at work?
- A) A cherry-picked time window
- B) A dual y-axis with two different scales
- C) A truncated y-axis that starts near 49,500 instead of at zero
- D) A 3-D pie chart tilted toward the viewer

**4.** Last summer Town A used 200 million gallons of water and Town B used 100 million. Town A has 50,000 residents; Town B has 10,000. Which statement does this information support?
- A) Town A's residents use more water each, since its total is twice as large
- B) Town B used more per resident, so "who uses the most?" depends on totals vs. per person
- C) The two towns can't be compared at all, because they're such different sizes
- D) Both towns used the same amount per person, since the bigger town has more people

**5.** You make a bar chart of the Max Weight column from the dog dataset and it's a mess — dozens of skinny bars, with nearly every weight getting its own bar. What does Lesson 4 suggest instead?
- A) Switch to a pie chart so every weight gets its own slice
- B) Make a histogram, which groups the weights into ranges (bins) and shows how many breed records fall into each range
- C) Remove rows from the dataset until there are fewer bars
- D) Make a crosstab chart, since weight is categorical data

**6.** At one elementary school, kids with bigger shoe sizes tend to have higher reading scores. It's a real, fairly strong correlation. Using Lesson 5's four possible explanations, which one best fits?
- A) A causes B — bigger feet make kids better readers
- B) B causes A — reading more makes kids' feet grow
- C) A third factor (a confounder) causes both — older kids have bigger feet and have also had more years of reading practice
- D) Pure coincidence — the two numbers just happen to line up

### True / False

**7.** Data cleaning is only necessary for huge datasets; a small table collected from a class survey can safely be charted exactly as it was entered.

**8.** Lesson 4 suggests that when you're tempted to use a pie chart to compare categories, you try drawing the same numbers as a bar chart too, since people compare lengths on a shared axis much more easily than angles and areas.

### Multiple Choice (continued)

**9.** Seven friends' commute times, in minutes, are 10, 12, 12, 15, 18, 20, and 95 (one friend lives out of town). The mean is 26 minutes and the median is 15. Which number better describes a typical commute in this group, and why?
- A) The mean, 26 minutes, because it uses every commute and is therefore always more accurate
- B) The median, 15 minutes, because the one 95-minute commute pulls the mean up but barely affects the middle value
- C) Neither, because the mean and median should always be equal for real data
- D) The mean, 26 minutes, because the median only works for categorical data

**10.** You want to know whether dog breeds with heavier maximum weights tend to have shorter maximum life spans. Which chart is the best choice, and why?
- A) A scatter plot, because it shows the relationship between two quantitative variables
- B) A pie chart, because each breed is part of one whole dog population
- C) A histogram, because it directly pairs each weight with its corresponding life span
- D) A bar chart, because bars are required whenever the data includes breed names
