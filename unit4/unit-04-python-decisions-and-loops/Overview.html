Unit 4 Overview - Functions, Decisions, and Repetition

In Unit 3 we got from an idea to a small running program. Those programs all had one thing in common: they ran straight through from top to bottom, and the only way to get different behavior was to give different input. This unit is where programs start to have some structure of their own. We'll give a group of steps a name (a function), let each use of it bring its own details (parameters), get results back out (return), ask yes-or-no questions, choose between paths, and repeat something for as long as a condition holds.

We start running into our first taste of real design decisions. A function's name can make clear what it does or hide it. A parameter list decides which variations a tool will even allow. Comparisons draw lines; if statements turn human rules into automatic outcomes; validation can help people or trap them. So we'll test whether our programs follow their rules, and we'll keep asking what those rules actually mean for the person on the other end.

Learning objectives

By the end of the unit, you'll be able to:

  1. Define and call functions, including functions with parameters and return values.
  2. Write and test Boolean expressions using comparisons and logical operators.
  3. Use if, elif, and else to create reachable, understandable branches.
  4. Use while for input validation or condition-controlled repetition and explain termination.
  5. Choose between for and while based on the reason repetition stops.
  6. Test functions, boundaries, branches, and loops with normal, boundary, and invalid cases.

Five-lesson path

  1. Functions as Named Actions: turn a repeated or meaningful block of code into something with a name, and call it more than once.
  2. Parameters and Return Values: let each call bring its own information, and hand results back to the program.
  3. Boolean Questions and Comparisons: make yes-or-no values and test them at the boundaries.
  4. Decisions and Logical Operators: choose among paths and turn compound rules into code that someone else can check.
  5. while Loops and Validation: repeat until a condition changes, and always leave a way out.

Learning activities and assessment

  1. Complete Lessons 1–5 and the Python Practice Problems.
  2. Complete Unit 4 Quiz.
  3. Complete one Week 4 creative-programming route: the ColabTurtle project or a text-first quiz/oracle or branching-radio-play notebook.
  4. Complete and share the Week 4 Activity Discussion: Personality Quiz.

Whichever Week 4 route you pick, you'll define a function of your own, call it more than once, and make at least one real decision with an if. Parameters and loops are there to make your creation richer. Whether it's a drawing or a text-based interaction is entirely up to you.
