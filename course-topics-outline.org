* Unit 1 — Digital Information
** Overview
- How computers store and represent complex information (numbers, text, images, sound)
- Building your own representation systems; compression and intellectual property as challenges of digitizing
- Objectives: describe how computers represent information; numbers in binary; bits for text and images; lossless vs. lossy compression; copyright and Creative Commons
** Lesson 01 - Computers and Information
- Computer science = study of information and information processes to solve problems ("computer scientists are problem solvers")
- Meaning of "information" and "information processes"
- The 4 processes every computer performs: input, store, process, output
- Hardware categories: CPU/processor; input devices (keyboard, mouse, webcam); output devices (speakers, monitor, printer); memory (RAM); storage (HDD, SSD, USB)
- Hardware vs. software; computer engineering as a related path
- Videos: "What makes a computer a computer?", "Hardware and Software"
** Lesson 02 - Circle Square Patterns
- Multiple ways to represent a quantity (tally marks, fingers, Roman numeral VII)
- Activity: invent a number system using only circles and squares with 3 place values; count distinct patterns; write the rules (Google Slides)
- Individual discussion: compare circle/square counting to everyday counting
** Lesson 03 - Binary Numbers
- Mapping circle/square patterns (3 place values) to quantities 0–7; counting from 0
- Counting rules: cycling through symbols and carrying on rollover
- Decimal system: place value, base 10, rollover from 9 to 0
- Binary system: two symbols (0/1), circle→0, square→1; "language of the computer"; two states (1/0, true/false, on/off)
- Light-switch analogy; a switch holds one bit; bits as electrical state on tiny silicon switches
- All information representable with on/off switches
- Video: "Circuits and Logic"
** Lesson 04 - Decimal to Binary
- Converting decimal↔binary; decimal place values as powers of 10; binary place values as powers of 2
- Paper "binary calculator" activity; Digital Binary Calculator (Google Sheets) toggling powers of 2
- Terms: bit (one 0/1), byte (8 bits); writing binary in groups of 8 with leading zeros
- Revisits circle/square activity to connect to 0s/1s
** Lesson 05 - Encoding Text
- Encoding text as an extension of encoding numbers (if it's a number, it can be bits)
- ASCII: encoding scheme; 'A' stored as 65; full ASCII table
- 7-bit ASCII = 128 values (0–31 control, 32–126 printable, 127 delete); transition to 8-bit chunk by adding a leading 0
- Abstraction: texting hides the ASCII→binary conversion; identify other everyday abstractions
** Lesson 06 - Black and White Images
- Representing pixels with bits; Code.org Pixelation Widget; drawing 'A' on a 3×5 grid
- Image metadata: 16 bits for width/height
- Analog (continuous) vs. digital; sampling; sample-size tradeoff (smaller samples = more detail; larger = blurrier)
- Counting bits and time to build sample images
- Individual discussion: report bits (incl. metadata) and time; pros/cons of more frequent sampling
** Lesson 07 - Color Images
- Pixels (picture elements) as colored squares; zoom-in example (Mario)
- RGB model: red/green/blue, each 8 bits, 0–255; 0 = off, 255 = full; RGB(0,0,0) black, RGB(255,255,255) white
- Worked examples; effect of scaling channels down (lighter/darker); W3Schools RGB calculator
- Review: how numbers/text/images are stored (video "Binary & Data")
** Lesson 08 - Lossless Compression
- Compression motivated by growing file sizes; everyday text abbreviations (brb, lol)
- Worked text-compression with a symbol dictionary/key; reconstructing original; counting characters (93 vs 56)
- Code.org Text Compression Widget; compression percentage; predicting easy vs. hard texts
- Lossless = no information lost, perfectly reconstructable; rate depends on strategy + patterns
** Lesson 09 - Lossy Compression
- Lossy text-compression widget (keep first letter, remove vowels); test readability
- Lossless vs. lossy contrast: lossy loses info, not reversible
- Lossy image compression example (silky bantam chicken, distortion); "Unraveling the JPEG" resource
- Choosing a compression level for a scenario
** Lesson 10 - Intellectual Property
- Intellectual property; you own what you create; plagiarism and legal consequences
- Copyright; when you can reuse/remix; Creative Commons licensing
- Three "Copyright & Creativity for Ethical Digital Citizens" videos
- Individual discussion ("Is Fortnite stealing black dance culture?"): what was digitized, purpose, who benefits/harmed, intended vs. unintended impacts
** Summary
- All information stored as 0s and 1s; the 4 computer tasks; bits/binary as a 2-symbol system; text via ASCII; color as R/G/B light; lossless vs. lossy; copyright and Creative Commons

* Unit 2 — The Internet
** Overview
- How the internet's design lets information be shared across billions of devices; problems the original designers solved
- Objectives: problems the internet solves; how its design enabled growth/evolution; protocol layers in a request; social dilemmas from its structure
** Lesson 01 - The Internet
- High-level overview; framing question: what happens when you type a web address
- Embedded video: "What is the Internet?"
** Lesson 02 - Internet Issues
- Who owns/controls the internet; cooperation vs. preventing IP/hostname conflicts
- ICANN; US government control of the domain hierarchy until 2009; questions of independence
- Open protocols (standards not owned by a company); IETF (consensus, not voting); ISOC
- English-only DNS until 2009; 2013 Snowden/NSA surveillance and US-control concerns
- History: ARPA/ARPANET (1968–69 first four university links), IP invented 1982, ARPANET decommissioned 1990, NSF network, rise of commercial IP networks
- Three societal issues: Net Neutrality, Internet Censorship, the Digital Divide
** Lesson 03 - Building a Network
- Designing a network: connect everyone, save money, plan for outages
- Graphs in CS (vs. statistical charts); cheapest way to connect every node; minimum spanning tree (cost 26)
- Shortest path between nodes; rerouting when a node fails; network graph simulator (graphonline.ru)
- Redundancy: multiple physical pathways; node vs. link failures
** Lesson 04 - Routers
- Routers pass info between networks; forward packets; choose fastest path
- Redundancy → reliability and scalability; routing = finding sender→receiver path
- Bandwidth: max data per fixed time, measured in bits per second
- ISPs (Xfinity, CenturyLink, Wave G); no single owner; USPS/post-office analogy
** Lesson 05 - Protocols
- Protocol suite; many device types needing to find/talk to each other; protocols as a "rule book"; open protocols
- Layered protocols and abstraction (request passes down, across, up)
- Link layer (WiFi); Internet layer (IP); Transport layer (TCP, UDP); Application layer (HTTP, DNS)
- Internet as the largest human artifact, reliant on cooperation
** Lesson 06 - Internet Protocol (IP)
- Battleship game to simulate building the internet; binary Battleship; designing an efficient binary protocol
- Protocol-design questions: addressing, names vs. numbers, where one address ends, what else is needed
- Group discussion: develop a 4-person Battleship protocol; encode sample plays
- IP: rules for routing/addressing packets; IP address as unique identifier; IPv4 (0–255 dotted) vs. IPv6; DNS resolvers
- Video: "IP Addresses & DNS"
** Lesson 07 - TCP Packets
- Transport layer: breaking messages into packets and reassembling; packets arrive out of order
- UDP: speed over accuracy (video conferencing, streaming, gaming); one-way
- TCP: accuracy over speed (email, photos, web); two-way; headers with ordering, acknowledgments, in-order reassembly
- Individual discussion: design a packet protocol (8 ASCII chars/packet) to reliably send a message
- Video: "Packets, Routing & Reliability"
** Lesson 08 - The Need for DNS
- IP addresses change; hard for each computer to keep a list; scaling to billions of devices
- DNS server as a "big phone book"; hierarchy of DNS servers
- DNS Lookup tool (mxtoolbox); finding the IP for www.pcc.edu; humans remember words better than numbers
- DNS not designed to be secure (security in Lab 2)
** Lesson 09 - HTTP
- HTTP/HTTPS; padlock/URL; HTTP as the protocol of the World Wide Web; HTML controls formatting
- Entering a URL sends an HTTP command to fetch/transmit a page
- SSL/TLS/HTTPS; certificate authorities verifying the intended site
- Client-server model; HTTP request structure (method GET/POST, resource, version, headers); response structure (version, status code, headers, body)
- How DNS + IP + HTTP work together to load a website
** Lesson 10 - The Cloud
- "The cloud" vs. "the internet"; history of slow bandwidth and large central computers; shift to personal devices
- Compute-heavy tasks sent to "computer farms" = the cloud; Google Docs example
- Video: "What is the cloud? | CNBC Explains"
** Summary
- Autonomous standard protocols; DNS converting URL→IP; standards + hierarchy enabling global scale
- End-to-end recap of a web request (DNS → HTTP GET → server HTML → TCP/UDP packets → IP routing → physical media)

* Unit 7 — Algorithms and Algorithm Efficiency
** Overview
- Writing/analyzing algorithms; recognizing common patterns; comparing sorting algorithms to illustrate efficiency
- Objectives: write/define algorithms; analyze searching and sorting; describe/evaluate efficiency; quantify with Big-O
** Lesson 01 - Introduction
- Algorithm = step-by-step instructions; "capturing intelligence"; everyday examples (pie recipe, directions, oil change)
- Five algorithm requirements: well-ordered; unambiguous (primitives); effectively computable; produces a result; halts in finite time
** Lesson 02 - Linear Search
- Searching large lists; linear search definition (check each item until found or end)
- Terms: index, key, list, location, =list[location]=; pseudocode; Python demo (random list)
- Weakness: may scan every item; doesn't scale (phone-book analogy)
** Lesson 03 - Binary Search
- Binary search on sorted lists; "guess the number" (1–15) intuition; always check the middle, discard half
- Constraint: requires sorted data; worked example (find 95 in 15 elements); midpoint formula =(low + high) / 2=
- Binary Search animation; "Guess the Number" program (=math.log= base 2 for max guesses, =userGuesses()=/=computerGuesses()=)
- Pros/cons of linear vs. binary
** Lesson 04 - Sorting Algorithms
- Sorting as a common problem (email by date/sender/&c.); preview insertion and selection sort
- Card-hand and array illustrations; two shared operations: comparison and swap (swap needs a temp cell = three copies)
** Lesson 05 - Selection Sort
- Concept: split into sorted/unsorted; repeatedly select smallest remaining and move to the front of the unsorted portion
- Human "Selection Card Sort" (8 steps); video walkthrough (sorted/unsorted divider)
** Lesson 06 - How Selection Sort Works
- Step-by-step statement; pseudocode (nested for loops, min-index tracking, conditional swap)
- Selection Sort animation; Python =selectionSort(arr)= with temp swap + iteration prints; example [12,10,7,6,3]
** Lesson 07 - Insertion Sort
- Concept: divide into sorted/unsorted; shift each card left until larger than its neighbor ("inserting")
- Human "Insertion Card Sort" (7 steps); cards swap step-by-step; video walkthrough
** Lesson 08 - How Insertion Sort Works
- Step-by-step (first element sorted; shift greater elements; insert); pseudocode (holePosition, valueToInsert, while shift)
- Insertion Sort animation; Python Tutor hand-trace
** Lesson 09 - Algorithm Analysis
- Multiple correct algorithms; measuring efficiency to choose
- Space efficiency (memory; swap needs one extra temp) vs. time efficiency (count copies/swaps/comparisons)
- Worked data on [7,8,5,2,4,6,3]: insertion 45 copies/19 comparisons; selection 15 copies/21 comparisons
- Insight: best sort depends on input order (counterexample where insertion beats selection)
** Lesson 10 - Selection Sort Analysis
- Worst-case analysis defined and why it's the standard
- Worst-case formulas: swaps = (n−1); comparisons = (n−1)+(n−2)+…+1; worked example n=6 → 15 comparisons
** Lesson 11 - Worst Case Comparisons
- Worst-case formulas for both sorts side by side; swaps→copies (×3)
- Tables for n = 10/100/1000/10000; closed form =(n*(n−1))/2=; insertion copies reveal an n² term (=(3/2)(n²−n)=)
- Selection has only an n term; "quadratic" terminology
- Individual discussion: research a better (divide-and-conquer) sorting algorithm
