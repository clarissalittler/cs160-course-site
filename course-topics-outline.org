* Unit 1 — Digital Information
** Overview
- Six-lesson path from computer systems through bits, standards, sampled media, compression, and responsible copying
- Every lesson is text-complete; videos, diagrams, spreadsheets, and widgets are optional alternate explorations
- Objectives: trace information through system layers; use bits, bytes, and binary; explain text/media representations; justify compression choices; distinguish ownership from permission; analyze who benefits, who may be harmed or excluded, and who decides
- Recurring social-impact lens in every lesson: who benefits, who might be harmed or left out, and who decides or should have a voice
- Week 1 Activity Discussion: choose Broken Poetry or Mona Lisa Underdrive and share the artifact plus technical and social reflection
** Lesson 01 - Computers as Layered Systems
- Computers perform four broad jobs with information: input, processing, storage, and output; one device can play different roles in different actions
- Hardware: CPU, RAM, persistent storage, and input/output devices; RAM vs. long-term storage
- Software: applications, operating systems, device software/firmware, and their relationship to hardware
- Programs as instructions, data as information acted upon, and the context-dependent boundary between them
- Abstraction as selective hiding of detail; tracing one camera action through application, OS, devices, processing, storage, and display
- Layered troubleshooting and accessibility: screen-reader example; technical design choices affect who can participate
- Everyday case: phone-app sensor permissions, useful features versus surveillance, and how power is divided among hardware, operating systems, developers, users, and institutions
- Optional videos: "What makes a computer a computer?" and "Hardware and Software"
** Lesson 02 - Bits, Bytes, and Binary
- Circle/square representation challenge, available through Google Slides or text/paper equivalents; patterns do not contain inherent meanings
- Bit as a two-state choice and why separated physical states support reliable systems
- Pattern rule: /n/ bits provide 2^/n/ patterns; unsigned maximum 2^/n/ − 1 when zero is included
- Byte as eight bits; distinction between file-size bytes and network-speed bits
- Binary place values as powers of two; complete worked conversions between binary 101101 and decimal 45
- Decimal-to-binary subtraction method; leading zeros and fixed-width fields
- Everyday cases: two-digit-year assumptions and the Y2K problem; a two-bit transit-fare field that cannot represent a new category; capacity is distinct from fair classification
- Optional Circuits and Logic video and binary-calculator spreadsheet
** Lesson 03 - Text, Unicode, and Standards
- Character encodings as agreements layered over numbers and bits; encoding vs. encryption
- Selected ASCII table; worked encoding and decoding; seven-bit ASCII and its English-centered limits
- Complete =Hi!= byte trace; UTF-8 examples for one-, two-, three-, and four-byte characters; concrete encoding mismatch and missing-glyph examples
- Unicode code points, UTF-8 bytes, and font glyphs as distinct layers; ASCII compatibility; normalization of equivalent sequences
- Grapheme clusters: one user-perceived character may contain multiple code points
- Standards as maintained social and technical infrastructure; language inclusion, compatibility, and participation
- Everyday case: names rejected, altered, or mismatched across school, medical, payroll, travel, and government systems
- Broken Poetry Activity Discussion connection: transform character numbers and interpret the resulting text
** Lesson 04 - Images and Sound
- Sampling continuous physical experience into finite measurements; frequency and precision tradeoffs
- Raster images: pixels, one-bit black-and-white examples, dimensions, resolution, row order, and metadata/privacy
- Color images: three eight-bit RGB channels, 0–255 values, 24-bit color, and #RRGGBB hexadecimal notation
- Sound: time-based samples, sample rate, bit depth, channels, and purpose-specific fidelity
- Worked raw file-size estimates for a 1920×1080 RGB image and one minute of stereo audio
- Optional Pixelation widget and RGB tool, with text equivalents
- Mona Lisa Underdrive Activity Discussion connection: reinterpret and alter image-file bytes as sound data
** Lesson 05 - Compression
- Raw media file sizes as the motivation for using fewer bits
- Lossless compression: byte-for-byte or sample-for-sample reconstruction; ZIP, PNG, FLAC, checksums, overhead, repetition/dictionaries, run-length example, and suitable scenarios
- Lossy compression: perceptual tradeoffs, screenshot/photo/audio comparisons, compounding generation loss, source/master/delivery workflow, and suitable scenarios
- Everyday case: platforms automatically choosing compression; access benefits versus lost intelligibility, detail, creator control, and trust
- Optional Text Compression widget and JPEG explainer, with text equivalents
** Lesson 06 - Copies, Ownership, and Permission
- Digital copying as an enabler of access, collaboration, and creativity and a source of compensation, context, and privacy tensions
- U.S. copyright fundamentals: original human authorship, fixation, expression rather than ideas/methods, automatic protection, and exclusive rights
- Ownership, work made for hire, transfer, physical ownership vs. copyright, and licenses as permission rather than ownership
- Reuse paths: unprotected material, public domain, license/permission, and statutory exceptions
- Fair use as a fact-specific four-factor analysis with no automatic educational exemption or safe percentage
- Creative Commons elements BY, SA, NC, and ND; six main licenses; CC0; attribution workflow
- Copyright distinguished from plagiarism, privacy/publicity, contracts/platform rules, trademark, and patent
- Dance-emote case: digitizing movement; legal protection of choreography versus ethical questions about cultural appropriation, attribution, compensation, context, and communal creation
- Generative AI stages separated into acquisition, training, and output/deployment
- AI-training fair-use analysis: the four factors, source lawfulness, output controls, market substitution and licensing; Copyright Office spectrum and fact-specific 2025 trial-court examples
- Competing social perspectives: creator consent and livelihood, research and public benefit, licensing and market concentration, community representation, and who has decision-making power
- Human authorship of outputs, digital replicas, voice/likeness harms, disclosure, platform/course policy, and ethical responsibilities
** Summary
- Synthesizes six paired ideas: layers/abstraction; patterns/interpretation; standards/language; sampling/measurement; compression/fidelity; copying/permission
- Explicit mechanism-and-human-decision table connects technical representations to design choices, audience, access, and responsibility
- Everyday consequences table and recurring social-impact lens connect app permissions, classification, names, compression, and copying at scale
- Readiness prompts cover system tracing, bit capacity, binary interpretation, sampling/file size, compression decisions, reuse permission, and stakeholder analysis

* Unit 2 — The Internet
** Overview
- Five-lesson path from the physical network of networks through packets, routing, layered protocols, the web/cloud/platform stack, and questions of access and governance
- Every lesson is text-complete; diagrams include meaningful alt text and full text equivalents when a graph or sequence carries information
- Objectives: distinguish internet/web/Wi-Fi/cloud/apps; explain packets/routing/performance/resilience; trace addresses/names/protocols and an HTTPS exchange; compare shared infrastructure dependencies; analyze an access or governance problem with technical and institutional responses
- Recurring social-impact lens in every lesson: who benefits, who might be harmed or left out, and who decides or should have a voice
- Week 2 Activity Discussion: Make Your Own Web Page (Neocities) as a practical demonstration of open publishing, accessibility, and remaining access barriers
** Lesson 01 - A Network of Networks
- Internet distinguished from Wi-Fi, the web, cloud computing, and an app or platform
- Ordinary course-page trace: device → local link → home/campus network → ISP and transit → destination network and server → response
- Networks join through shared protocols; local networks, ISPs, interconnection, client and server roles
- Physical infrastructure: radio, copper, fiber, undersea cables, routers, data centers, electricity, cooling, construction, and labor
- Failure-scope diagnosis: device/local link, local network/ISP, provider path, or destination service
- Corrected history: ARPANET resource sharing and communication; packet switching; later survivable internetworking across unlike radio, satellite, and fixed networks; 1983 TCP/IP transition
- Everyday case: no single internet owner, but landlords, ISPs, institutions, clouds, standards groups, and governments control parts of the connection chain
- Neocities connection: a student can be a web publisher rather than only a platform consumer
** Lesson 02 - Packets, Routing, and Resilience
- Packet switching compared with dedicated circuits; bursty computer communication sharing link capacity
- Header, payload, and encapsulated transport data; IP headers distinguished from TCP sequence information
- Store-and-forward routing; routers choose next hops; routes reflect technical conditions and organizational policy
- Network graph with complete accessible edge list; lowest-cost C→I path and rerouting after node E fails
- Redundancy as alternate capacity that costs money; minimum-connectivity efficiency distinguished from resilience
- IP best effort; loss, duplication, delay, and reordering; TCP acknowledgments/retransmission/congestion response; UDP's smaller service and application-defined tradeoffs
- Throughput versus latency; queues, congestion, drops, and sending-rate adjustment
- Resilience limits: shared conduits/power/upstreams, convergence delay, absent backup capacity, endpoint failure, and intentional filtering
- History presented as overlapping goals: resource sharing, efficiency, interoperability, robustness, public research, academic collaboration, and military funding
- Everyday case: institutions can buy multiple providers and tested backup power while many households have one connection and limited cellular data
** Lesson 03 - Addresses, Names, and Protocols
- Protocols define formats, meanings, sequence, errors, and beginning/end behavior; fixed-width design tradeoffs
- Complete text-based four-player Battleship protocol: player/row/column encodings, message type, result, and sample shot/reply bit strings
- Four-layer TCP/IP model: application, transport, internet, and link; abstraction and encapsulation
- IPv4 and IPv6; dynamic and multiple addresses; private IPv4 and NAT; an address as a delivery identifier rather than automatic proof of one person's identity
- Ports as software endpoints and the combination of transport protocol/address/port
- DNS as a distributed hierarchical database; resolver cache, root referral, top-level domain, authoritative server, and returned record
- Domains as administered identifiers; registrant/registrar/registry/ICANN roles; DNS dependence, observation, and blocking
- Open RFC specifications enabling independent implementations; formal openness distinguished from equal practical ability to participate
- Everyday case: shared or changing IP addresses can lead to mistaken attribution; DNS filtering can protect people or suppress lawful information
** Lesson 04 - The Web, Cloud, and Platform Stack
- Web as one internet service; URL, HTTP, HTML, browser, and independently operated servers
- URL scheme, host, and path; host routing versus application resource selection
- Complete web trace: URL parsing → DNS → routing/transport → TLS → HTTP request/response → rendering and additional resources
- Conceptual HTTP GET and 200 response; status codes as diagnostic evidence; link to the public HTTP RFC
- HTTPS confidentiality, integrity, and domain authentication in transit; limits involving endpoints, metadata, DNS, malicious sites, managed-device inspection, and destination logging
- Web-server software distinguished from hosting and cloud computing; remote infrastructure remains physical, owned, powered, cooled, and governed
- CDNs, caches, origin servers, performance/security benefits, and hidden shared dependencies
- Platforms' accounts, discovery, moderation, payments, audiences, and data; concentrated policy power and exit costs
- Fastly June 2021 incident from the provider's postmortem as a case of correlated failure rather than the entire internet failing
- Data-center electricity, water/material, land, labor, and community impacts; dated DOE estimate marked for later verification
- Roads not taken: Gopher and Gemini as examples of alternate values and protocol choices
- Neocities connection: HTML page as an independently linkable personal resource; semantic headings, useful link text, alt text, and keyboard access
** Lesson 05 - Access, Power, and Governance
- Layer-by-layer power map: standards groups, ICANN/registries/registrars, ISPs, clouds/CDNs/hosts, platforms/app stores, governments/courts, users/communities
- IETF open drafts, working groups, rough consensus, implementation, and RFC review; participation barriers despite formal openness
- ICANN's limited multistakeholder role coordinating global unique identifiers; governance as standards, contracts, technical coordination, markets, law, and community action
- Digital divide expanded beyond coverage: availability, affordability, quality, suitable devices, skills/support, accessibility, safety/trust/relevance
- Dated NTIA 2023 access figures and smartphone-only/device disparities; statistics flagged for future updating
- Community-college access examples: locked/shared computers, installation privileges, file management, browser-based tools, and smartphone limitations
- Accessibility as meaningful access: alt text, keyboard interaction, captions, assistive technology, and equivalent routes
- Visibility map: endpoints, local network/ISP, DNS resolver, destination, and platform; encryption changes trust and observation rather than eliminating them
- Blocking at DNS/address/port/application/hosting/platform/app-store layers; safety, moderation, censorship, proportionality, transparency, appeal, and bottleneck power
- Net-neutrality principle, reasonable network management, arguments about innovation/speech/investment/regulatory power, and dated July 2026 U.S./California legal status
- Technical and institutional response pairs for cable cuts, cloud outages, overbroad DNS blocks, and inaccessible course tools
- Synthesis studio: diagnose a DNS problem, affordability gap, shared-provider outage, or platform-removal case; name path/evidence/decision-maker/stakeholders/mitigations/uncertainty
** Week 2 Assignment — Understanding Internet Problems
- Part 1 supplies seven web-request events for students to order by when each process begins, then asks them to explain four selected events with an optional vocabulary bank
- Part 2 restores the Wordle protocol problem: calculate a fixed-width five-letter guess, then design and explain a binary reply for turns remaining and three possible results per letter; multiple defensible encodings are accepted
- Part 3 uses separate prompts to analyze a DNS failure, affordability gap, shared-provider outage, or platform decision through evidence, central and contributing factors, who benefits/is harmed/decides, one technical response, one institutional response, and remaining uncertainty
- Diagram optional; written explanation required so the assessment is not vision-dependent
** Week 2 Activity Discussion — Make Your Own Web Page (Neocities)
- Browser-based setup with no local installation privilege required; students may use a nickname and share the live link in D2L
- Personal topic with semantic heading structure, original paragraphs, meaningful outbound link, image, and useful alt text
- Optional CSS exploration; link, alt-text, and keyboard checks
- Reflections on a personal corner of the web, image accessibility, who benefits from free browser hosting, one remaining barrier, and who could reduce it
- Peer exchange: visit and respond constructively to at least two classmates' pages
** Summary
- Synthesizes five paired ideas: physical interconnection; packet sharing/resilience; layered identifiers and protocols; encrypted web/cloud/platform dependencies; access and governance
- Mechanism/benefit/social-question table connects routes, identifiers, HTTPS, clouds, and governance to evidence and affected people
- Diagnosis method pairs a precise technical layer with the responsible organization and combines a technical mitigation with an institutional response
- Readiness prompts cover network/request traces, packet delivery/performance, identifiers/layers/DNS, HTTPS limits, shared dependencies, and stakeholder analysis

* Unit 4 — Functions, Decisions, and Repetition
** Overview
- Reuse and responsiveness: name processes, vary calls, return results, ask Boolean questions, choose paths, and repeat until a condition changes
- Objectives: functions/parameters/returns; comparisons/logical operators; conditionals; =while=; =for= versus =while=; tests for calls, boundaries, branches, and termination
- Social thread integrated throughout: abstraction can clarify or conceal; categories and thresholds are human choices; validation and exits affect users
** Lesson 01 - Functions as Named Actions
- Review calls to existing functions; distinguish an existing call from a student-defined function
- =def= syntax, descriptive verb-style name, empty parameter list, colon, and indented body
- Definition records a recipe; an unindented call performs it
- Function called more than once for a radio-play transition or repeated turtle element
- Functions and loops solve different problems and compose cleanly
- Abstraction, single coherent responsibility, maintenance, and collaboration
- Social thread: a confident function name can conceal consequential assumptions as easily as it can clarify intent
** Lesson 02 - Parameters and Return Values
- Parameter in a definition versus argument in a call; positional matching
- One and several parameters; repeated calls with different values
- Local names and call boundaries at an intuitive level
- Printing to a person versus returning a value to the surrounding program
- Function contract: expected inputs, returned result or visible effect, and limitations
- Informal tests call a returned calculation with normal and boundary values
- Social thread: a parameter list determines which forms of variation a tool permits
** Lesson 03 - Boolean Questions and Comparisons
- =True= and =False= as values produced by comparison expressions
- Operators =====, =!===, =<=, =<==, =>=, and =>==; assignment ===== versus equality ======
- Below/at/above boundary tests; one character can change who qualifies
- Exact string comparison and normalization with =.strip().lower()=
- Boolean variables and question-style functions such as =is_matinee(hour)=
- Social thread: thresholds turn continuous situations into categories and require technical and policy scrutiny
** Lesson 04 - Decisions and Logical Operators
- =if= for optional execution; =if=/=else= for two paths; =if=/=elif=/=else= for several
- Indentation, first-true branch behavior, branch order, and fencepost tests
- =and=, inclusive =or=, =not=, and readable parenthesized compound rules
- Decision table translates a community tool-library policy into inspectable cases
- Functions that return different results from different branches
- Social thread: code may implement a rule correctly without making the rule fair; affected people need explanation, correction, and appeal
** Lesson 05 - =while= Loops and Validation
- Condition-controlled repetition and comparison with the counted =for= loop learned in Unit 3
- Initialization, condition, update, pretest behavior, tracing, and termination argument
- Infinite-loop diagnosis focuses on variables in the condition and direction of change
- Text-response validation with normalization and =and=
- Numeric range validation, with an explicit statement that nonnumeric text still needs later exception handling
- Sentinel loop and priming read; sentinel is checked but not accumulated
- Social/accessibility thread: validation explains constraints and preserves a clear exit rather than blaming or trapping the user

* Unit 5 — Collections, Data Models, and Interfaces
** Overview
- Move from isolated values to collections and records representing a small part of the world
- Browser-compatible ordinary Python; text examples carry full meaning
- Objectives: sequences/lists, collection-processing patterns, dictionaries/records, schemas/missingness, and accessible interface evaluation
** Lesson 01 - Strings as Sequences
- String as ordered immutable sequence; =len()=, indexes, negative indexes, and stop-exclusive slices
- Direct traversal versus index traversal
- Transformation by building a new string
- Methods: =.strip()=, =.lower()=, =.upper()=, =.replace()=, =.startswith()=, =.split()=
- Preserve original human-entered form when normalization is only for comparison
- Writing-system caveat: Python elements and visible grapheme clusters are not always identical
** Lesson 02 - Lists and Changing Collections
- Create, index, slice, measure, append, insert, replace, and remove
- Membership as a safe precondition for removal
- Empty collections and invalid indexes as expected states
- Direct element loops versus index loops
- Intuitive aliasing: two names can refer to one mutable list; full slice for a simple copy
- Modeling thread: collection names need precise definitions of what belongs
** Lesson 03 - Patterns for Processing Collections
- Traversal, transformation, filtering, aggregation, counting, and min/max
- Build a new collection rather than destroying source data unintentionally
- Initialize min/max from a real element; handle empty input
- Separate calculation from input/display with functions
- Return =None= when no result exists rather than confusing absence with zero
- Testing: empty, one-item, typical, and boundary collections
- Interpretation: filters and summaries direct attention and omit information
** Lesson 04 - Tuples, Dictionaries, and Records
- Tuple as an ordered immutable sequence; indexing, slicing, membership, and the one-element comma
- Packing and unpacking; matching arity; concise swaps
- Functions return a tuple when several results form one value

* Unit 7 — Algorithms and Algorithm Efficiency
** Overview
- Writing/analyzing algorithms; recognizing common patterns; comparing sorting algorithms to illustrate efficiency
- Objectives: write/define algorithms; analyze searching and sorting; describe/evaluate efficiency; quantify with Big-O
** Lesson 01 - Introduction
- Algorithm = step-by-step instructions; "capturing intelligence"; everyday examples (pie recipe, directions, oil change)
- Five algorithm requirements: well-ordered; unambiguous (primitives); effectively computable; produces a result; halts in finite time
** Lesson 02 - Linear Search
- Searching large lists; linear search definition (check each item until found or end)
- Terms: index, key, list, location, =list[location]=; pseudocode; Python demo (random list)
- Weakness: may scan every item; doesn't scale (phone-book analogy)
** Lesson 03 - Binary Search
- Binary search on sorted lists; "guess the number" (1–15) intuition; always check the middle, discard half
- Constraint: requires sorted data; worked example (find 95 in 15 elements); midpoint formula =(low + high) / 2=
- Binary Search animation; "Guess the Number" program (=math.log= base 2 for max guesses, =userGuesses()=/=computerGuesses()=)
- Pros/cons of linear vs. binary
** Lesson 04 - Sorting Algorithms
- Sorting as a common problem (email by date/sender/&c.); preview insertion and selection sort
- Card-hand and array illustrations; two shared operations: comparison and swap (swap needs a temp cell = three copies)
** Lesson 05 - Selection Sort
- Concept: split into sorted/unsorted; repeatedly select smallest remaining and move to the front of the unsorted portion
- Human "Selection Card Sort" (8 steps); video walkthrough (sorted/unsorted divider)
** Lesson 06 - How Selection Sort Works
- Step-by-step statement; pseudocode (nested for loops, min-index tracking, conditional swap)
- Selection Sort animation; Python =selectionSort(arr)= with temp swap + iteration prints; example [12,10,7,6,3]
** Lesson 07 - Insertion Sort
- Concept: divide into sorted/unsorted; shift each card left until larger than its neighbor ("inserting")
- Human "Insertion Card Sort" (7 steps); cards swap step-by-step; video walkthrough
** Lesson 08 - How Insertion Sort Works
- Step-by-step (first element sorted; shift greater elements; insert); pseudocode (holePosition, valueToInsert, while shift)
- Insertion Sort animation; Python Tutor hand-trace
** Lesson 09 - Algorithm Analysis
- Multiple correct algorithms; measuring efficiency to choose
- Space efficiency (memory; swap needs one extra temp) vs. time efficiency (count copies/swaps/comparisons)
- Worked data on [7,8,5,2,4,6,3]: insertion 45 copies/19 comparisons; selection 15 copies/21 comparisons
- Insight: best sort depends on input order (counterexample where insertion beats selection)
** Lesson 10 - Selection Sort Analysis
- Worst-case analysis defined and why it's the standard
- Worst-case formulas: swaps = (n−1); comparisons = (n−1)+(n−2)+…+1; worked example n=6 → 15 comparisons
** Lesson 11 - Worst Case Comparisons
- Worst-case formulas for both sorts side by side; swaps→copies (×3)
- Tables for n = 10/100/1000/10000; closed form =(n*(n−1))/2=; insertion copies reveal an n² term (=(3/2)(n²−n)=)
- Selection has only an n term; "quadratic" terminology
- Individual discussion: research a better (divide-and-conquer) sorting algorithm
** Lesson 12 - Big-O
- Order notation; highest-order term dominates; Big-O = growth-class of a function
- Categories: Constant, Linear, Logarithmic, Quadratic; notation/pronunciation (O(n²), O(n), O(log n), O(2ⁿ))
- Result: selection and insertion sort are both O(n²)
** Lesson 13 - Search Efficiencies
- Linear search: best O(1), average n/2 → O(n), worst n → O(n); overall O(n)
- Binary search: discard half each step; worked worst-case trace on 1000 items (10 comparisons)
- Logarithms (base-2: how many halvings to reach 1); binary search is O(log n)
- Worst-case comparison table (linear vs. binary for sizes up to 1,000,000,000); growth graph
** Unit 7 Summary
- Algorithms as the heart of CS; searching large lists; many sorting algorithms; Big-O recap (Constant/Linear/Logarithmic/Quadratic)
