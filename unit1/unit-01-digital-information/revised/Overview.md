# Unit 1 Overview

![Unit 1 banner](img/banner.jpg)

In this unit, we're going to explore how computers store and represent complex information --- numbers, text, images, sound, the works. It's kind of wild when you think about it: everything your computer does boils down to patterns of ones and zeros. By the end of this unit, you'll understand *how* that's possible (and why it's both elegant and occasionally maddening).

We'll start by investigating what it even means to "represent" information. You'll get to build your own representation systems --- which is more fun than it sounds, I promise --- and then we'll look at how real-world systems handle the same problem. Along the way, we'll bump into some genuinely interesting challenges: how do you compress all that data? And who owns a digital creation, anyway?

## What we're covering

**After this unit, you'll be able to:**

1. Describe how computers represent information.
2. Represent numbers using the binary number system.
3. Explain how bits can be arranged to represent text and digital images.
4. Determine whether lossless or lossy compression should be used for a given situation.
5. Explain how copyright and Creative Commons Licenses apply to digital works of creativity.
