## A quick thought experiment

Let's say you want to buy 7 items. How many different ways can you *represent* the quantity 7?

<details>
<summary>Show Solution</summary>

There are lots of different ways! You could use 7 tally marks, hold up 7 fingers, write the Roman numeral VII, draw 7 little stars, use the word "seven," tap the table 7 times... the list goes on. The point is that "7" isn't the number itself --- it's just one of many possible *representations* of that quantity. That distinction is going to matter a lot.

</details>

## Circle square patterns

Okay, here's the fun part. What if you could *only* represent quantities using circles and squares? No digits. No tally marks. Just two shapes.

Open this [Google Slide Deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1AdDJ2DsC6tZ5pYutoZwp095rLZz_cxzYMo_tO1LqcE8/template/preview) --- that's your workspace. Your job is to figure out how many different patterns you can make with only circles and squares using **3 place values**. Follow the directions in the slide deck, and write down the "rules" you develop to create your patterns.

This might feel a bit like a puzzle. That's because it is one. Lean into it.
