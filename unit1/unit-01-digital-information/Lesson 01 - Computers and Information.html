What is a computer?

You might assume computer science is the study of computers themselves - the physical machines we have on our desks and carry around in our pockets - but it's really the study of information, and of how we process information to solve problems. Computer scientists are problem solvers first, machine-owners second. So in this lesson, we're going to think about what "information" and "information processes" actually mean.

As you watch the video below, keep two questions in the back of your mind:

What makes a computer a computer?

Every computer — from a supercomputer to the chip in your microwave — does four basic things with information: it takes information in (input), holds onto it (storage), transforms it (processing), and sends the results back out (output). Keep those four in your back pocket — you'll see them again and again all term.
