In binary it is the same, except for each place value is a power of 2 and we can only use 1's and 0's in each place. In the video below, you will be creating a paper binary calculator with an 8.5 x 11 inch piece of blank paper. 

Decimal to Binary

Here is the Digital Binary Calculator referenced in the video. Turn on and off the power of 2 by checking the check box. Sum all of the place values with 1's to find the decimal equivalent. 

Each 1 or 0 is called a 'bit' and 8 bits is called a 'byte'. We usually write binary numbers in groups of 8, adding leading 0's to the number if needed.
