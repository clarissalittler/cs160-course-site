## What's an interpreter?

The only way to learn how to code is to actually *code*. There's no way around it. It can feel tricky at first --- like learning to ride a bike where the handlebars are a keyboard --- but the more you practice, the more natural it becomes.

To write and run Python programs, you'll need an **interpreter**: a program that reads your code, figures out what it *means*, and then *runs* it. Think of it like a translator who speaks both English and Python --- you write something, the interpreter reads it, and then tells the computer what to do.

## Your options

We've got a few recommendations depending on your setup and preferences.

### Online option

- [Online GDB](https://www.onlinegdb.com/) --- This one runs right in your browser, which is super convenient. It supports *tons* of languages, not just Python, and you can save and share your projects if you make a free account. The downside? Ads. But hey, it works.

### Offline options

- **IDLE** --- This comes bundled with Python itself, so once you install Python, you already have it. It's bare-bones but gets the job done.
- **PyCharm** --- A more fully featured development environment. This is our top recommendation --- read on for details.

## PyCharm (our recommendation)

Our current recommendation is to install the [Community Edition of PyCharm](https://www.jetbrains.com/pycharm/download/), which is completely free. When you're on the download page, make sure to scroll down and grab the version labeled **PyCharm Community Edition** --- don't accidentally download the paid one.

PyCharm is a great choice if you want to program offline or if you just want a really solid coding environment. The main thing to keep in mind is that since it's on your local computer, you'll want to back up your files to Google Drive, Dropbox, your email inbox --- whatever you prefer --- so you don't lose your work.
