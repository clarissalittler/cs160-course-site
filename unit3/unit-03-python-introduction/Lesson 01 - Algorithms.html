Algorithms

Computers are designed to do any job that their programs tell them to do. Remember, all computers can do 4 tasks: take input, store data, process data, and give output.

A program is a set of instructions, called an algorithm, a computer follows to perform a task. Programs are written in programming languages. Programs are commonly referred to as software or code.

Algorithms


If problem-solving is a central part of computer science, then the solutions that you create through the problem-solving process are also important. In computer science, we refer to these solutions as algorithms.

An algorithm is a step-by-step method for accomplishing some task. In computer science, algorithms are precise sequences of instructions for processes that can be executed by a computer and are implemented using programming languages. Let’s practice writing an algorithm with virtual Lego’s!

Problem-solving

Algorithms are steps to solve a problem. Hopefully, you discovered the importance of including detailed steps and not leaving anything for inference! Here is an example of a problem we can solve with a computer. 

Problem: Write an algorithm to find and print the minimum of two numbers, x and y.

Algorithm:

  1. if x is smaller than y
  2.    print x
  3. else
  4.    print y

When we look at the numbers 2 and 5 written on a piece of paper, we intuitively know that 2 is smaller than 5. Behind that intuition is a familiarity with numbers we have acquired over years of experience both in and out of school. Computers do not have that sort of intuition. They must perform mathematical calculations to figure out which of the two numbers is smaller.

After we have developed our algorithm, it is important to test to make sure it works with all cases. When comparing two numbers, what could the outcomes be?

  • x could be smaller than y
  • y could be smaller than x
  • x and y could be the same number

Choose test data to test the algorithm:

  • x = 2 and y = 4
  • x = 4 and y = 2
  • x = 2 and y = 2

Run through the algorithm above with all three scenarios. Does our algorithm work?
