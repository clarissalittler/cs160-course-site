# Unit 7 Summary

## Wrap Up

In this unit, we learned how to write and analyze algorithms by talking about what makes a good algorithm tick. We also compared several sorting algorithms to see how algorithm efficiency and analysis work in practice.

## What We Covered

- **Algorithms** are step-by-step instructions to solve a problem --- they're the heart of computer programming and computer science.

- Computers spend a *lot* of time working with large lists of data. One of the fundamental tasks they do with that data is **search** it for particular values --- say, looking up a specific student record so it can be displayed. The simplest approach? Linear search --- just check every item, one at a time.

- Dozens of different algorithms have been written to solve the sorting problem. With such a wide range of solutions, how do we figure out which one is best? That's where analysis comes in.

- **Big-O** classification is the tool we use to compare algorithms: we assign each one to a class of functions that describes how its work grows. The common categories we saw were **Constant**, **Linear**, **Logarithmic**, and **Quadratic** --- and the differences between them are enormous.
