# Unit 10 Summary

## Wrap Up

In this unit, we learned how data privacy, security threats, and encryption all fit together into the bigger picture of cybersecurity. We looked at how personal data gets collected and monetized, how attackers use social engineering and malware to exploit people and systems, and how tools like strong passwords, multi-factor authentication, and encryption help keep your data safe. We also examined real-world data breaches to see what happens when those defenses fail.

> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Privacy policies govern how your data is collected, used, and sold --- but they're often designed to be difficult to read. Understanding what you're agreeing to is the first step toward protecting yourself.
> - Social engineering --- phishing, pretexting, baiting, &c. --- is the *most common* attack vector. The weakest link in any security system is usually a human being, not a piece of software.
> - Encryption is what makes secure communication possible. Symmetric encryption uses a single shared key; public key cryptography solves the problem of exchanging keys safely over an insecure channel.
> - Strong, unique passwords combined with multi-factor authentication are your best practical defense against unauthorized access to your accounts.
