Unit 10 Summary

Wrap up

You came into this unit maybe a little worried about hackers, and you're leaving with something better than worry: a game plan. The throughline was that staying safe online isn't about being a tech genius - it's about a handful of habits and a healthy pause before you click, believe, or share. You can't be unhackable, but you can absolutely be a harder target than the next person, and that's most of the battle.

Summary

  • You're a target because crime is a numbers game - but online safety is habits, not hacking, and your job is just to be a harder target than the next person.
  • Scams work by manipulating people, not computers. They pull emotional levers - urgency, authority, fear, greed, sympathy - so the cure is to slow down and verify through a channel you choose yourself.
  • Spot fakes by reading the URL (watch for lookalike and mismatched domains), and treat QR codes, unexpected "alerts," and even AI-cloned voices and video as unverified until you check.
  • Your feed is engineered for engagement, not truth - misinformation spreads because it's emotional, and AI makes fakes cheap to mass-produce.
  • Check before you trust or share using the SIFT moves: Stop, Investigate the source (read laterally), Find better coverage, and Trace claims and images to the original.
  • Lock down your accounts with a unique password for every site (let a password manager remember them), turn on two-factor authentication, and use passkeys where offered.
  • Mind your privacy: you leak data just by living online; trim app permissions, manage your settings, and decide deliberately what's known about you.
  • Protect yourself from harassment by limiting exposure, and know that if it happens it isn't your fault - document, block, report, and get help.
  • When something goes wrong, don't panic: keep software updated and backed up, and act fast - change passwords, enable 2FA, document, and report.
