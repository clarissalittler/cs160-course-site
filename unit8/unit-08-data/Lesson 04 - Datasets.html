Visualizations

Hopefully, we can answer these questions using visualizations! Why do people make visualizations out of data? In the picture below, what is the intersection of 'Max Life Span' and 'Australian Shepherd' telling us? It is telling us Australian Shepherds have a Max Life Span of 16 years.

There is a lot of data in the spreadsheet. The bar chart in figure 8.8 shows us the number of breeds with a specific Max Life Span. The x-axis tells us the number of years and the height of the bar tells us the counts, or number of dogs with Max Life Span of that year. You can estimate the counts by looking at the height of the bar compared to the number on the y-axis. From this chart we can see:

  • A Max Life Span of 15 years has the highest number of counts at 25 breeds.
  • There are only approximately 2 dog breeds with a Max Life Span of 8 years.
  • No breeds have a Max Life Span of 9 years.
  • (22 + 13 + 23 + 25 = 83)  83 out of 105 total breeds (look at the number of rows in the spreadsheet) or 83/105 = 79% of dog breeds have a Max Life Span between 12 and 15 years.

Visualizations can help us look at lots of data at once and see patterns that are "invisible" if you just look at the table.

We are going to learn how to make different types of visualizations to answer different types of questions!

Data analysis process

The data analysis process consists of the following steps:

  1. Collect or choose the data
  2. Clean the data
  3. Visualize and find patterns
  4. Obtain new information
