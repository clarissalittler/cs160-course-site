## Chart Types

You've now got a whole toolkit for visualizing both categorical and quantitative data. The trick is knowing which tool to reach for. Here's how to think about it:

**Are you working with one column of data?**

- **Bar Chart:** Bar charts display and compare the number, frequency, or other measures (like a mean) for different discrete categories. They answer questions like "how many?" and "what percentage?"
- **Histogram:** Similar to a bar chart, but a histogram groups numbers into ranges. The height of each bar shows how many values fall into that range. Reach for a histogram when a bar chart would have too many individual categories. It answers "how many are between *this* and *that*?"

**Are you trying to show the relationship between two columns?**

- **Scatter Plot:** Scatter plots work best when you're comparing lots of data points and want to show the relationship between two variables (the x and y axes) --- for example, a person's weight and height. (Are heavier people also taller?) You can also use them to make a range chart when one column is categorical.
- **Crosstab:** Best when at least one column is categorical data (text). Answers questions like "how many people have green eyes?" or "what percentage of working breeds live past 12?"
