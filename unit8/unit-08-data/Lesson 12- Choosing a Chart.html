Chart types

Now you have the tools to visualize both categorical and quantitative data. Here are considerations for choosing the correct chart type:

Are you using one column of data?

  • Bar Chart: Bar charts are a type of graph that is used to display and compare the number, frequency, or other measures (e.g. mean) for different discrete categories of data. (How many? What percentage?)
  • Histograms: A graphical display of data using bars of different heights. It is similar to a Bar Chart, but a histogram groups numbers into ranges. The height of each bar shows how many fall into each range. Used when a bar chart has too many categories. (What percentage/how many are between this and that?)

Are you trying to show the relationship between two columns of data?

  • Scatter Plot: A scatter plot works best when comparing large numbers of data points and is trying to show the relationship between two variables (x and y-axis), for example, a person's weight and height. (Are people heavier people also taller?) Can also be used to make a range chart when one column is categorical.
  • Crosstab: One column must be categorical data (text). (How many/what is the percentage of people who have green eyes?)
