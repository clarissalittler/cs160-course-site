Crosstab

A crosstab (short for cross-tabulation) chart counts how many times each combination of values shows up in a dataset. See what you can figure out from the crosstab chart below - try these questions:

  1. How many Herding breed records list a maximum life span of 12 years?
  2. What is the most common maximum life span for "Working" breeds?
  3. Which group's recorded maximum life spans tend to be lowest?
  4. Which group's recorded maximum life spans tend to be highest?
  5. How do you know? How confident are you in your answers?
Show Solution

All of the questions can be answered from the chart. Here's how:

  1. 4 Herding breed records list a maximum life span of 12 years. Find the "Herding" row, slide over to the "12" column, and read the count in that cell.
  2. 12 years. In the "Working" row, the biggest count is 8, and it sits in the 12-year column - more Working breeds have a max life span of 12 than any other value.
  3. Working leans shortest. The only 8-year breeds in the whole table are one Hound and one Working breed, but look at where the Working row bunches up: 16 of its 23 breeds (1 + 6 + 1 + 8) have a max life span of 12 years or less, and no Working breed goes past 15. That is 16/23, or about 70%, of the Working row. Compare proportions within each group rather than raw counts, because the group sizes differ.
  4. It depends what "highest" means! The single highest recorded maximum is 20 years, for one Terrier breed. But Toy records cluster at the high end: 1 at 17 and 2 at 18, so 3/12 or 25% exceed 16 years. For Terrier the corresponding proportion is 1/10 or 10%. One highest value and a concentration of high values answer different questions. State which one you're answering.
  5. Moderately, with caveats. These answers describe this dataset of 105 breed records, not all dogs everywhere. Their interpretation depends on the number of records and the measure chosen: the Working row contains 23 records, while the Terrier row's 20-year maximum comes from one record out of 10. The Mixed group contains only two records, which supports very little generalization.

Information from crosstab charts

So, to sum up: crosstab charts count how often pairs of values from two columns appear together.

Useful for:

  • Finding the most / least common combinations of values in two columns
  • Finding patterns across two columns
  • Exploring two columns when one or both are strings (text)
  • Discrete data (counts)

Not useful:

  • If either column has too many values (the chart would be enormous)

Seaborn

Seaborn, the chart library from Lesson 1, can turn a crosstab into a heatmap, where each cell is colored by its count. Your eye finds the dark and light patches far faster than it finds the big and small numbers in a plain grid.

Open this Dogs Crosstab Colab Notebook and make a copy. Run the program to see how it works. What other questions can you answer about Breed Groups? 

To investigate a different column, swap in its name as shown below:

counts = pd.crosstab(index=df["Breed Group"], columns=df["Max Life Span"], margins=True)
print(counts.to_string())

Change the column name after columns= to explore another variable. The printed table carries the same counts as the heatmap, without relying on its colors.
