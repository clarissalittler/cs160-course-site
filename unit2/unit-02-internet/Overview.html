Unit 2 Overview

This unit is about one big idea: the internet was built to be distributed - spread out, with no center - so that it could survive losing any single piece. You'll learn how that distributed design actually works (packets, redundancy, addresses, and layered protocols, kept at a citizen's level, not an engineer's).

Then you'll follow the tension that runs through the whole modern internet: the powerful pull to re-centralize it onto a handful of giant companies - which makes it cheaper and faster, but also more fragile and easier to control. Along the way you'll look at who gets access and who doesn't, and who holds power over the pipes everyone now depends on.

Learning objectives

At the end of this learning module, you will be able to:

  1. Explain the internet as a distributed "network of networks," and how packets and redundancy let it survive damage. 
  2. Describe how addresses, names (DNS), and layered protocols move information across the internet. 
  3. Explain the pull toward centralization (cloud, CDNs, platforms) and why it makes the internet convenient but brittle. 
  4. Discuss the social stakes of the internet - the digital divide and who controls the pipes. 

Learning activities

You are expected to do the following tasks to achieve the above objectives:

  1. Complete Lessons 1 - 8 (LOs 1-4)
  2. Complete Quiz 2 (LOs 1-4)
