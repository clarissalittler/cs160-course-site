What is the internet?

There are a lot of pieces to the internet which we will cover in this unit! Figure 2.1 shows an overview of the content you will be learning in this module. When you type in a web address into your browser, what happens next? How does the information get displayed on your computer screen?

Here's the first surprise: the Internet isn't one big machine, and nobody owns it. It's a network of networks — the college's network, your phone carrier's network, Netflix's network, and thousands of others — all agreeing to pass each other's messages along using the same set of rules. No single company or government is in charge of the whole thing. It works because everyone plays by the same protocols, which is a genuinely remarkable thing when you stop to think about it.

So what actually happens when you type in a web address? Roughly, your message hops from your device to your router, out across a chain of networks that hand it off to one another, until it reaches a server — some computer, maybe on the other side of the planet, that has the page you asked for. Then the answer makes the whole trip back to you. All of that happens in the time it takes you to blink, and this unit is going to slow it down and walk through it piece by piece.

Along the way we'll answer some big questions. How does a message find the one right computer out of billions? How does it arrive in one piece when it's been chopped up and sent across all those different networks? And who runs all of this, anyway? Hang on to those questions — each lesson chips away at them.
