Careers in AI

AI isn't just a career for computer scientists anymore. Almost every field — from healthcare to music to law — needs people who understand AI. You don't need a PhD to work with AI (though some of these roles involve one). Here are some of the paths people are taking.

Click on the title in the sections below to find a description and skills needed for each of the career examples. Also listed are links to people currently working in that positions. As you browse these careers, think about which ones surprise you. You don't need a computer science degree for all of them.

AI researcher

What they do

  • AI researchers are at the cutting-edge of trying new ideas in AI
  • Most work in labs at universities, though there are some company labs as well

Skills required

  • Interest in a subject
  • Creative thinking - you are coming up with new things to try
  • Ability to document and communicate your new ideas to others
  • Study other research

AI/ML engineer

What they do

  • Super wide variety of jobs, depending on the branch of AI/ML
  • The "meat" of AI/ML - they write the software that makes things work
  • Many of them were / also are researchers
  • Where a researcher researches new AI / ML techniques, Engineers implement them.

Skills required

  • Math, in particular Statistics
  • Programming Skills
  • Problem-solving - breaking down pattern matching understanding variables
  • Learning how to learn - ML is a constantly changing field; there is a lot to keep up with learning. Do you learn best from books/blogs? Conferences? Building things?

AI ethicists

This is also a brand new job description! It is closely related to AI policy, but where Policymakers work with governments, ethicists often work directly with companies. Often, people with this job description are also the AI Engineers.

What they do

  • Create guidelines for a company to follow
  • Consider risks of new AI software and how to mitigate risks
  • Connect to policymakers and customers

Skills 

  • Solid understanding of AI
  • Publications in AI ethics, research

NLP Career

Prompt engineer

This role barely existed before 2023. Prompt engineers design the instructions, examples, and workflows that get the best results from large language models. Think of it as learning how to communicate effectively with AI.

What they do

  • Design and test prompts for LLM-powered applications
  • Fine-tune AI behavior for specific tasks (customer service, code generation, content creation, &c.)
  • Document best practices and create prompt libraries for teams

Skills

  • Clear, precise writing — you're communicating with a system that takes you literally
  • Understanding of how LLMs work (see Lesson 6!)
  • Patience and systematic experimentation
  • Domain knowledge in whatever field you're applying AI to

AI safety researcher

People working to make sure AI systems behave as intended and don't cause harm. This includes everything from making sure a chatbot doesn't give dangerous medical advice to thinking about the long-term implications of increasingly capable AI systems.

What they do

  • Test AI systems for unexpected or harmful behavior (sometimes called "red-teaming")
  • Research alignment — how to make AI systems do what we actually want
  • Develop safety benchmarks and evaluation frameworks

Skills

  • Critical and creative thinking — you need to imagine failure modes others haven't considered
  • Technical understanding of AI systems
  • Ethics and philosophy background is genuinely useful here
  • Communication skills — explaining risks to non-technical stakeholders
