Lesson 2 - What Do People Mean by AGI?

At the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain the difference between "narrow" and "general" artificial intelligence.
  • Identify different meanings of "artificial general intelligence" that are frequently conflated

General vs. Narrow

Narrow artificial intelligence means an AI program that has a very specific task it's meant to solve. A computer vision program that's supposed to detect cars in an image is narrow AI. A program that predicts housing prices based on past data is narrow AI. A program that plays a specific video game is narrow AI. 

Basically everything we have today counts as narrow AI. This gets a little fuzzy with things like ChatGPT and similar large language models (LLMs). 

A general AI would be something that applies to many tasks, a program that can adapt and be used for new problems that it was never meant to be used for. This distinction, though, is still a little different than what people tend to mean when they say "Artificial General Intelligence" (AGI)

What is AGI?

It turns out this is a difficult question to answer because people generally mean several different things when they say AGI. Consider the following definitions:

  • AGI is when you have invented an AI program that can apply to any problem domain, transferring skills it has learned and adapting to new domains. AGI would be a program that could equally be used to help optimize a factory's production or develop speedrunning strategies for a videogame.
  • AGI is when an AI is human-competitive (or better!) in a broad range of tasks
  • AGI is when an AI is a conscious, living thing, like us

The more you read about how people talk about AGI the more you'll find that people can slip between these different definitions without being clear that they're doing it! 

Is ChatGPT AGI? Well, by the first definition it's pretty close! By the last definition, absolutely not! 

Since this question was first posed, models have gotten dramatically more capable. GPT-4, Claude, Gemini — they can write essays, debug code, and pass bar exams. By some people's first definition, we might already be there. By the third definition, we're not even close. The goalposts keep moving, and that's part of the point.

Whenever you read about AGI, try to figure out which of these three classes of definition the author is using.

Something to think about:

When you hear the term "AGI" in the news, which of the three definitions above do you think the speaker means? How can you tell?
