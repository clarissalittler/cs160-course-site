Unit 9 Summary

Wrap up

You came into this unit with AI as a black box and, hopefully, you're leaving with the box pried open. The big trick was never magic: a model is a machine with knobs, "learning" means turning those knobs to shrink a mistake, and everything from a line of best fit to ChatGPT is that same idea at wildly different scales. Once you've seen a ball roll into a valley and watched blame ripple backward through a tiny network, the giant models stop being sorcery and start being engineering - impressive, powerful engineering, built and steered by people. That understanding is exactly what makes you an informed citizen rather than a passive user.

Summary

  • AI today is mostly machine learning - and most everyday AI is "narrow" (great at one task), not the general, human-like AI (AGI) of science fiction.
  • Machine learning learns from examples rather than hand-written rules: show it labeled data (supervised), or let it find patterns on its own (unsupervised), or train it with feedback/reward (reinforcement).
  • The heart of learning is one loop: guess, measure the mistake (the "wrongness"), use gradient descent to roll the knobs downhill toward less wrongness, and use backpropagation to figure out which way to turn every knob at once. Repeat millions of times.
  • Neural networks are giant stacks of simple "weigh-add-fire" neurons; stacking many layers ("deep" learning), plus huge data and fast hardware, set off the deep learning revolution of the last decade-plus.
  • The recent boom came from training enormous models (transformers / large language models) to predict the next word at internet scale - GPT-3, then ChatGPT and the rest - tuned to be helpful with human feedback (RLHF).
  • Generative AI is powerful but fallible: it predicts plausible output rather than looking up facts, so it can be confidently wrong ("hallucinate") - verify what matters.
  • AI inherits human bias from its data, so the questions who benefits, who's harmed, who decides? matter as much as the technology. There are careers here for every major, not just programmers.
