Unit 9 Summary

Wrap up

You've covered a lot of ground in this unit — from what AI actually is (and isn't) all the way through the inner workings of large language models and the real ethical questions that come with deploying these systems in the world. The big takeaway? AI isn't magic, and it isn't science fiction. It's math, data, and design choices made by people. Understanding how it works — even at a high level — puts you in a much better position to use it thoughtfully, spot its limitations, and think critically about how it shapes the world around you.

Key Takeaways

  • AI is prediction and decision-making, not consciousness. When we talk about artificial intelligence today, we're talking about systems that learn patterns from data and use those patterns to make predictions or decisions. Nobody's built a thinking, feeling machine — current AI is powerful but narrow.
  • Machine learning means learning from data, not following hand-written rules. Instead of a programmer spelling out every step, ML systems are given examples and figure out the patterns on their own. That's a fundamentally different approach to building software.
  • There are three main types of machine learning: supervised learning (labeled examples), unsupervised learning (finding structure in unlabeled data), and reinforcement learning (trial and error with rewards). Each is suited to different kinds of problems.
  • Neural networks changed the game by learning their own features. Deep learning lets the system figure out what to pay attention to in the data, rather than requiring humans to hand-pick those features. That's what made breakthroughs in image recognition, language, &c. possible.
  • Large language models are next-word prediction engines. LLMs like ChatGPT work by predicting the most likely next token in a sequence. The attention mechanism lets them keep track of context, and the context window determines how much text they can "see" at once. They're impressively fluent, but they don't understand the way you do.
  • Generative AI creates new content — it doesn't search a database. When an AI generates text, images, or music, it's producing something new based on patterns learned during training. It's not copying and pasting from a filing cabinet somewhere.
  • Bias and ethics: garbage in, garbage out — with real consequences. AI systems learn from data that reflects the world as it is, including its biases and inequalities. That means biased training data leads to biased outputs, and those biases can cause real harm in hiring, healthcare, criminal justice, &c.
  • AI is already woven into your daily life. From autocomplete on your phone to recommendation algorithms on streaming services to spam filters in your email — you interact with AI systems dozens of times a day, whether you realize it or not.
  • There are diverse and growing careers in AI. The field needs more than just programmers. AI researchers, data scientists, ML engineers, ethicists, policy experts, NLP developers, AI artists — there's room for people with all kinds of backgrounds and interests.
