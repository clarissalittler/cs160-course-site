Unit 9 Summary

Wrap up

This unit opened part of AI's black box. A model contains adjustable parameters; training changes those parameters to reduce a measured error. The line-fitting example, loss landscape, and backpropagation interactive made that process visible at a small scale. Large systems add far more data, parameters, computation, and design choices, but they remain engineered systems built and directed by people. Understanding that distinction is part of being an informed user and citizen.

Summary

  • AI today is mostly machine learning - and most everyday AI is "narrow" (great at one task), not the general, human-like AI (AGI) of science fiction.
  • Machine learning learns from examples rather than hand-written rules: show it labeled data (supervised), or let it find patterns on its own (unsupervised), or train it with feedback/reward (reinforcement).
  • A central training loop makes a prediction, measures the loss, uses backpropagation to calculate gradients, and uses an optimizer such as gradient descent to update the parameters. The process then repeats.
  • Neural networks combine weighted inputs through layers. Deeper architectures, large datasets, improved methods, and faster hardware all contributed to the deep-learning advances of the 2010s.
  • The growth of large language models combined transformer architectures, next-token prediction, extensive training data, computing resources, and additional tuning. RLHF is one method for tuning behavior using human preferences.
  • Generative AI is powerful but fallible: it predicts plausible output rather than looking up facts, so it can be confidently wrong ("hallucinate") - verify what matters.
  • AI inherits human bias from its data, so the questions who benefits, who's harmed, who decides? matter as much as the technology. There are careers here for every major, not just programmers.
