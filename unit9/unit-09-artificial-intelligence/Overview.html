Unit 9 Overview

This unit examines artificial intelligence, including the technology behind chatbots, image generators, and many less visible tools. Rather than treating AI as magic, we will look at the mechanisms underneath it.

The first half develops one central idea: a machine can learn by adjusting internal numbers to reduce its errors. We will follow that idea from fitting a line through data to training a neural network with gradient descent and backpropagation. The second half considers what those methods have produced: deep learning, large models, practical uses of generative AI, and questions about bias, ethics, and decision-making.

By the end, AI should look less like magic or menace and more like a collection of mathematical methods, data, and human choices.

Learning objectives

At the end of this learning module, you will be able to:

  1. Explain what AI is today, and the difference between narrow AI and general AI (AGI).
  2. Explain how machine learning learns from examples instead of hand-written rules.
  3. Describe, in plain terms, how a model learns - gradient descent and backpropagation tuning a network's "knobs."
  4. Describe what neural networks and deep learning are, and how the deep learning revolution and the recent boom in large models came about.
  5. Use generative AI tools thoughtfully, and discuss the societal impacts, bias, and ethics of AI.
