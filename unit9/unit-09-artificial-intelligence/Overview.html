Unit 9 Overview

This unit is about artificial intelligence: the technology behind chatbots and coding agents, and also behind plenty of less flashy tools, like spam filters and route finders, that you use every day without thinking about it. Instead of treating AI as magic (or as a monster), we're going to look underneath and see how it works.

We'll start with what "AI" has meant over the years, then follow one central idea through the middle of the unit: a machine can learn by adjusting numbers to make fewer mistakes. We'll watch that happen with a one-knob model and a tiny neural network. Then we'll see what happened when that idea met huge amounts of data and computing power: deep learning, transformers, chatbots, and agents that can take actions on their own. The last lesson asks our three questions about all of it.

By the end, I hope AI looks less like magic or menace and more like what it is: math, data, and a long chain of human choices.

Learning objectives

At the end of this learning module, you will be able to:

  1. Explain what AI has meant over its history, and the difference between narrow AI and the contested idea of general AI (AGI).
  2. Explain how AI, machine learning, and neural networks fit together, and how machine learning learns from examples instead of hand-written rules.
  3. Describe, in plain terms, how a model learns: gradient descent and backpropagation tuning a network's "knobs."
  4. Describe deep learning, transformers, large language models, and AI agents, and use generative AI tools thoughtfully.
  5. Discuss the social impacts of AI, from bias in narrow systems to the risks of powerful agents.
