Unit 9 Overview

This unit is about artificial intelligence: the technology behind chatbots and coding agents, and also behind plenty of less flashy tools, like spam filters and route finders, that you use every day without thinking about it. Instead of treating AI as magic (or as a monster), we're going to look underneath and see how it works.

We'll start with what "AI" has meant over the years, then follow one central idea through the middle of the unit: a machine can learn by adjusting numbers to make fewer mistakes. We'll watch that happen with a one-knob model and a tiny neural network. Then we'll see what happened when that idea met huge amounts of data and computing power: deep learning, transformers, chatbots, and agents that can take actions on their own. The last lesson asks our three questions about all of it.

By the end, I hope AI looks less like magic or menace and more like what it is: math, data, and a long chain of human choices.

Learning objectives

At the end of this learning module, you will be able to:

  1. Explain what AI has meant over its history, and the difference between narrow AI and the contested idea of general AI (AGI).
  2. Explain how AI, machine learning, and neural networks fit together, and how machine learning learns from examples instead of hand-written rules.
  3. Describe, in plain terms, how a model learns: gradient descent and backpropagation tuning a network's "knobs."
  4. Describe deep learning, transformers, large language models, and AI agents, and use generative AI tools thoughtfully.
  5. Discuss the social impacts of AI, from bias in narrow systems to the risks of powerful agents.

Lesson map

The seven lessons in Unit 9
LessonCentral ideaQuestions to carry forward
1. What Is AI?The name AI covers a long history of different ideas, from programs built for a particular task to the contested idea of general intelligence.What is this system actually able to do? What would someone have to mean by 'intelligent' for the label to fit?
2. Learning from ExamplesMachine learning adjusts a model using examples, instead of spelling out every rule by hand. There are several ways to supply those examples and feedback.Where did the training examples come from? How could we test the model on cases it hasn't seen?
3. Fitting a Line and Rolling DownhillWe can measure how far a model's answers are from the examples, then adjust its numbers to try to make those errors smaller.What counts as an error? How does changing a parameter affect it?
4. Neural Networks and BackpropagationA neural network combines many adjustable numbers to produce an answer. Backpropagation works backward from an error to help work out how to adjust them.What travels forward through the network? Why does each weight need its own adjustment?
5. Deep Learning and TransformersDeep networks stack layers of learned calculations; transformers use a technique called attention. These are among the ideas behind language models.What has a language model been trained to predict? How could we check an answer that sounds convincing?
6. AI AgentsGive a model tools and a way to use their results in a loop, and it can attempt tasks that take more than one step. That's the basic idea behind an AI agent.Which actions can the agent actually take? What evidence would show whether it completed the task?
7. AI and SocietyAI systems can be useful, and their training and use can also cause or repeat harms. The outcomes depend on choices made by people and institutions.Who benefits or bears the costs? Who can question, govern, or change the system?
