Question 2: Design a Compact Protocol

Imagine that you are designing a protocol for a Wordle-like game. Each guess contains exactly five English letters. The server replies with the number of turns left, from 0 through 5, and one of three results for each letter: not in the word, in the word but in another position, or correct position.

a. If one letter is encoded using the smallest fixed-width field that can represent 26 possibilities, how many bits are required for a complete five-letter guess? Show your reasoning.

b. Using separate fixed-width fields, how many bits are required for the server reply? Include both the turns-left field and all five letter-result fields. Show your reasoning and note any unused bit patterns.

c. A bit layout alone is not a complete protocol. Add one rule that would help the client and server handle a lost, duplicated, or delayed message. Explain the problem your rule solves.

Question 3: Diagnose an Incident and Map Its Stakes

Choose one scenario:

  1. A course site opens by its known IP address but not by its domain name on one campus network.
  2. A neighborhood has one advertised broadband provider, but the monthly charge, installation fee, and required equipment are unaffordable to many residents.
  3. Several unrelated news and shopping sites return errors at the same time, while email and small independent sites continue to work.
  4. A creator's page disappears from a large platform after a moderation decision, but the creator's personal website remains reachable.

Write an incident explainer of roughly 250–400 words. Include all six parts:

  1. Path: a text trace of the relevant technical layers and organizations;
  2. Evidence: what works and what fails;
  3. Diagnosis: the most likely failing layer or decision-maker and one plausible alternative;
  4. People: who benefits from the normal design, who is harmed or excluded in this incident, and who decides;
  5. Responses: one technical mitigation and one institutional, policy, or organizational response; and
  6. Uncertainty: one additional fact you would seek before acting.
