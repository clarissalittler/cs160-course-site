Week 2 Rubric — Understanding Internet Problems

Score each row independently. Half-points are welcome. Add the five scores (20 points possible), then divide by 5 for the 0–4 assignment score.

Criterion 4 — Complete and accurate 3 — Mostly demonstrated 2 — Partly demonstrated 1 — Beginning evidence 0 — No evidence
Web-request sequence (1A) Gives a defensible conceptual order from URL interpretation through DNS, routing, TLS, HTTP, and display. One event is misplaced while the overall dependency chain remains clear. Several events are misplaced, but some important dependencies are correct. Provides an order with at least one defensible relationship. No relevant sequence.
Web-step explanations (1B) Explains four selected steps accurately and identifies who or what performs each. Four explanations are present with one thin or inaccurate entry. Two or three steps are usefully explained, or four show partial understanding. One step has a relevant explanation. No relevant explanations.
Wordle protocol (2A–b) Correctly sizes the guess and designs a decodable reply with sufficient fields, patterns, and total bits. The design is usable with one minor size, total, or explanation error. One part is correct and the other has a meaningful field/bit setup. Shows a relevant powers-of-two or message-field attempt. No relevant protocol work.
Evidence, diagnosis, and uncertainty (3A–C, 3F) Uses at least two clues, distinguishes supported conclusions from alternatives, identifies the central factor, and names a useful next fact. Reasoning is mostly evidence-based but one requested element is thin. Uses some scenario evidence but diagnosis, alternative, or uncertainty is underdeveloped. Names a plausible issue or clue with little support. No relevant analysis.
People, power, and responses (3D–E) Identifies concrete beneficiaries, burdens, decision-makers, plus fitting technical and institutional responses with explanations. Covers all areas with one vague or weakly matched element. Several relevant stakeholders or responses are identified but important connections are missing. Names at least one relevant stakeholder or response. No relevant work.

Partial-credit notes: ordinary language earns full credit when technically accurate. Do not deduct twice when the same misconception affects a sequence and its explanation; score the accurate evidence visible in each row.
