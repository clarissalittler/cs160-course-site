# Week 1 Rubric — Digital Information

Score each row independently. Half-points are welcome. Add the five scores (20 points possible), then divide by 5 for the 0–4 assignment score.

| Criterion | 4 — Complete and accurate | 3 — Mostly demonstrated | 2 — Partly demonstrated | 1 — Beginning evidence | 0 — No evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trinary conversion (1a–b) | Both conversions are correct and work is visible. | Both methods are substantially correct with one arithmetic/digit error, or both answers are correct with little work shown. | One conversion is correct, or both have useful setups but unresolved errors. | A relevant conversion attempt is present. | Neither part is attempted or relevant. |
| Trinary capacity (1c–d) | Correctly distinguishes the number of 5-trit patterns from the largest representable value and answers both. | Both ideas are present with one minor error or thin explanation. | One answer is correct or the distinction is partly understood. | Uses a relevant power/place-value idea but does not reach a defensible answer. | No relevant work. |
| ASCII extension (2a–b) | Finds the total needed characters, chooses the minimum sufficient bit width, and gives its capacity. | Correct bit width and capacity with one small reasoning or arithmetic gap. | One part is correct or both parts show a sound powers-of-two setup. | Identifies that powers of two determine capacity but makes limited progress. | No relevant work. |
| Unicode capacity (3a–b) | Totals the character needs, selects the minimum sufficient bit width, and gives the correct maximum capacity. | Sound method and nearly correct results with one minor error. | One part is correct or a useful lower/upper-bound calculation is shown. | A relevant power-of-two attempt is present. | No relevant work. |
| Reasoning and communication | Place values, powers, and units are shown clearly enough to follow throughout. | Reasoning is shown for most answers but one area relies on unsupported results. | Some useful work is visible, though several answers are hard to follow. | Minimal explanation accompanies at least one relevant answer. | Only unrelated material or no work is present. |

Partial-credit notes: score correct subparts even when their paired subpart is wrong; an arithmetic slip after a correct setup should lose less credit than a missing or conceptually unrelated method.
